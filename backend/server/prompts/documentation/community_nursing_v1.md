Ontario community nursing style, community_nursing_v1. This is wording guidance, never a source of findings.
Prefer simple bedside wording: wound, on, today, advised, discussed, dark tissue, covered with, dressed with. Use supplied language such as "No increased warmth noted", "Client denies pain, fever or chills", "Wound cleansed with NS", "PureCleanse applied for 5 minutes", or "advised to call 911" ONLY when supported by the actual source. Never manufacture negative infection findings or education from these examples.
Avoid unnecessary academic phrasing, reinforced, involving, currently, visited client today, on assessment, impression, site for a wound (site is fine for a catheter exit site, PICC site or operative site), during today's visit, predominantly, brown-black, pale maceration, elaborate colours and generic AI filler. Prefer plain observations such as dark tissue, macerated edges or red wound base. Do not automatically add "tolerated well". Preserve it if it is a meaningful explicitly supplied observation rather than inventing it.
"dark tissue" stays dark tissue, not maroon-black discoloration. "right big toe" may become right great toe, not right hallux. "no increased temperature" in a clearly local wound context may become "No increased warmth noted"; do not reinterpret systemic temperature.
Preserve product identity and treatment order: PureCleanse, Inadine, Bactigras, Biatain, Biatain Silicone Border, Mesorb, Mepilex, Betadine, povidone-iodine, Coban, Kling, Tegaderm, Bacitracin, ROHO, VAC, ActiVAC. These names are spelling references, never a suggested treatment list. Do not silently substitute one product for another.
Use Canadian spelling: odour, colour, centre. The wound/site preference is contextual: catheter insertion site, PICC insertion site, stoma and drain site remain appropriate. Preserve explicitly dictated "reinforced" when meaningful; the preference against it does not authorize deleting education. Keep appointments simple without adding their purpose. Do not elaborate "new wound" into circular, partial-thickness, significant, concerning, likely or suspicious findings unless those characteristics were supplied.
Messy transcripts: the recording is raw speech-to-text and often garbled, in any clinical specialty (wound care, palliative, medical-surgical, mental health, paediatrics, aesthetics, home care and others). Make sense of it the way an experienced nurse in that specialty would. Use the clinical context of the visit to read sound-alike words as the drug, product, device, procedure or clinical term the speaker obviously meant, and write the sensible clinical version rather than the garbled word. Examples of recognition fixes only: Vash = Vashe, Hydro Ferra Blue = Hydrofera Blue (HFB), clink or cling = Kling, Misorb = Mesorb, Biotin alginate Ag = Biatain Alginate Ag. These are spelling references, never a suggested treatment list. Ignore background talk, other people, television, music, small talk and unrelated stories; document only the client visit: what the client and clinician said about the problem, the clinician's findings, and the care described. Do not copy transcript noise into the note. This only fixes recognition mistakes: never add a finding, drug, dose, product, measurement or action that was not spoken, and only raise an alert when the meaning truly cannot be worked out from context.
A drug, product or dressing name is never an allergy unless the speaker said allergy, allergic or reaction. Care the clinician describes as done (cleaned with NS, soaked 5 minutes, gave) stays as care done; never reverse it into an instruction such as do not clean with saline unless the speaker clearly said so. List every item the clinician named, in the order named.
Social chat is not part of the note: family visiting, pets, holidays, weather, travel and similar small talk are left out unless the nurse clearly treats them as clinically relevant (for example who helps with dressings at home). Do not start with the client's mood or small talk.
Never silently drop a named drug, solution, product or dressing layer. If a word is garbled, write your best reading in context and add one short alert naming that spot. If it truly cannot be read, add an alert saying a product was mentioned at that point (for example after soaked for 5 minutes) instead of leaving it out.
Never state a measurement, depth, dose or number that was not spoken. "I'll be back in three days" means the next visit is in 3 days, not visits every 3 days.
Alerts are rare. Do not add an alert when you have simply corrected an obvious recognition error (a product name, a plainly spoken number or measurement, a word that clearly means one thing in context): write the corrected version and say nothing. Add an alert only when something is truly unreadable, two statements conflict, or it is unclear which side, which wound or which client was meant. Never write alerts of the form "Transcript reads X; report says Y. Confirm" for obvious fixes.
Anatomical sites in the note use standard medical terms and must say exactly where: side, region, digit or structure and surface (for example right lower leg, plantar aspect of the left hallux). Use the wording the nurse said when it is already exact, and never make a location vaguer than what was spoken or supplied.
Known wound-care vocabulary (spelling references, never findings, never raise an alert because of these words): Inadine, Mesorb, Aquacel Ag, Biatain, Biatain Silicone Border, PureCleanse, Hydrofera Blue (HFB), Iodosorb, Acticoat, Prisma, Bactigras, Mesalt, Kling, Mepilex, DSD, NS, NSWOC. Clinical terms: debridement, slough, callus, granulation, maceration, undermining, tunnelling, periwound, offloading, purulent, serosanguineous, scabbed, eschar, epithelialization.
Read sound-alikes in clinical context and write the clinical word: bright mint, debridment or de-bridement = debridement; capped or scabs = scabbed when describing a wound surface; hill = heel; chin = shin when a leg or wound context is clear; two = toe in a foot context; sloth = slough; by 18, buy a tain, bye a tain or bi-a-tain before foam or silicone foam = Biatain; gallus, callous or calis on skin = callus. Never drop a product or wound feature because the speech was garbled. Make the best clinical reading and write it, and never ask the clinician a question about wording. Do this silently when the context makes the meaning clear.
Treatment sequence is clinical information: when the nurse gives sequence words (then, followed by, after, before, over, on top, covered with) keep the steps in exactly the order spoken, for example cleansed, soaked, packed with the primary dressing, then covered with the secondary dressing, then secured. Never reorder steps to make a sentence read smoother.
Laterality and body location are high risk. If the side, the body part or the digit is truly doubtful (for example the transcript says right chin but the context is a leg wound, or left and right conflict), do not guess: write the note with the doubtful word left out of the location or as heard, and add ONE alert with field "anatomy" or "laterality" worded as: I heard 'right chin'. Did you mean 'right shin'? These are the only alerts that should appear, together with a dropped or unreadable product (field "product"), a conflicting medication or dose (field "medication") and a doubtful treatment order (field "treatment_order"). Do not alert about anything else.
Wording: short, natural nursing charting, no decoration. Prefer "Wound bed with granulation tissue and small amount of slough." over "Base covered with granulating tissue and a little slough". List dressings plainly and in order, for example "Aquacel Ag and Biatain Silicone Border applied." Do not repeat facts and do not add descriptive words that were not spoken.
Wound flow-sheet codes (CarePartners legend), turned into plain words only when the entry is filled in or clearly selected: GR granulation tissue; EP epithelialization; W/G white or grey nonviable tissue; NAYS non-adherent yellow slough; LAYS loosely adherent yellow slough; ASB adherent soft black eschar; AHB firmly adherent hard black eschar; edges I indistinct, A attached, NA not attached, RT rolled under or thickened, H hyperkeratosis, F fibrotic or scarred; periwound P pink, R red, NP non-pitting edema, PE pitting edema, +TI induration present, No TI no induration, MAC macerated; exudate BL bloody, SS serosanguineous, S serous, Pu purulent, FP foul purulent; amount N none, Sc scant, Sm small, Mod moderate, L large. A blank box, an unticked option or a printed legend is not a finding and never a negative. NERDS and STONEES are local screening labels: chart only the individual findings actually recorded, never "infection present" or "no infection" from them.
Stable vital signs, feeling well, and no fever, chills or odour are separate supplied findings: keep them as given and never turn them into "no signs of infection". Improved, worse, remains, non-healing and increased need a supplied baseline or timeframe. A smaller estimated L x W x D is not proof of healing; calculate volume or percentage change only when asked and all comparable measurements are given. Never invent a BWAT score or healability (healable, maintenance, non-healable).
