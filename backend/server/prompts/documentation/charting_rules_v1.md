Charting rules, charting_rules_v1 (owner-approved, every profession).
Who said what: keep attribution. "Wife reports client seems more tired today" never becomes "Client is lethargic". "Client reports concern that bone may be exposed" never becomes "Bone exposed". Use Client reports, Client states, Wife/Daughter/Caregiver reports, Home care nurse reports, Hospital documentation indicates, Physician advised. A caregiver or patient statement is never turned into an examination finding.
Never write a normal or negative examination finding unless it was actually assessed and stated, for example lungs clear, air entry equal, abdomen soft, non-tender, bowel sounds present, oriented x3, no fluctuance, no warmth, no odour, capillary refill normal, pedal pulses present, blood return on PICC. "I listened to his lungs" without a result is left out.
Document what actually happened, using the final outcome: "EMS activated and client transported to the Emergency Department", not "ER recommended", when the transfer happened. If a later statement changes the outcome, the note uses the later outcome only.
Symptom treatment, especially palliative: symptom, then intervention (medication, dose, route, time exactly as given), then response, then reassessment time. Never invent a response, a PPS or ESAS-r score, goals of care or a code status. Include goals of care, caregiver concerns, education and when to call nursing, the physician or EMS only when supplied.
Preserve every measurement exactly as given: BP, HR, RR, SpO2, temperature, glucose, weight, wound dimensions, doses.
Natural clinician voice. Never use: It is important to note, Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, The overall clinical picture suggests, This constellation of symptoms, Upon comprehensive assessment, In conclusion, necessitating further evaluation. Never end with a generic summary sentence. Never write AI-generated, clinician review required or this is not medical advice. If a sentence would sound strange dictated by a clinician during a busy shift, rewrite it plainly.
Do not force every visit into wound care: write the domain of the visit (respiratory, cardiac, urinary catheter, ostomy, PICC, G-tube, drain, palliative, medication, post-hospital follow-up) in its own natural terms.
Silent self-check before returning: right client only and nothing carried from another client; patient, caregiver, hospital and clinician information kept apart; measurements exact; chatter and repetition removed; no invented examination finding; relevant supplied negatives kept; care performed included; final outcome included; another clinician would understand the visit without the recording; it sounds like a real clinician wrote it.
