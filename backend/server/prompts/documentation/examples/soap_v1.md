Style examples for SOAP (invented composite cases). Copy the voice and structure only, never any finding, product or plan.
Example A
S: Client seen for chronic left heel wound present for about 3 years after a chemical burn. Reports fluctuating pain radiating into the foot, tolerable to severe at times. Reports sensitivity to silver products and adhesives causing burning and periwound irritation. Previous vascular assessment reported no significant findings. Denies fever or chills.
O: Chronic ulcer to posterior/lateral left heel with moist pink-red wound bed and adherent yellow-white slough. Edges irregular. Periwound fragile with moisture-related skin damage and mild erythema. No purulent drainage, malodour or spreading cellulitis noted.
A: Chronic non-healing inflammatory heel ulcer with significant periwound sensitivity. No clinical signs of acute infection.
P: Continue current dressing as ordered and protect periwound from adhesives. Monitor for increased redness, warmth, swelling, drainage, odour, pain or fever.
Example B
S: Client seen for left 3rd toe and left shin wounds, wound care q3days. Reports throbbing foot pain 4 to 5 times daily when the compression foot component is tightened, relieved by loosening. Previous ABPI reported within normal limits. Denies fever or chills.
O: Left 3rd toe with dry adherent black/brown necrotic eschar to distal tip and nail edge, surrounding dry hyperkeratotic tissue and iodine staining. Toe mildly edematous and shiny with localized redness. No active drainage, purulence or malodour. Left shin superficial wound with pink-red moist bed and small amount of serous drainage.
A: Dry stable necrotic eschar to left 3rd toe, high risk due to distal location and possible ischemic component despite normal ABPI. Compression-related throbbing pain raises concern for poor fit or distal perfusion concern. At risk for infection, wet gangrene and further tissue loss if necrosis progresses.
P: Continue current dressings as ordered. Keep toe eschar dry and protected; avoid debridement unless ordered and vascular status confirmed. Reassess compression fit. Notify physician regarding toe eschar and compression intolerance.
