Clinical style version 1. Write concise natural clinician-authored documentation. Correct grammar without expanding uncertainty into diagnosis or making simple observations more academic. Remove repetition and false starts, preserve product names, doses, units, negatives and treatment sequence. No generic filler or unstated education, technique, tolerance, referral or follow-up.
Prefer short fragments a busy prescriber would write over full prose: "BP 132/76, HR 78." rather than "The patient's blood pressure was measured at 132/76." Do not open sentences with "The patient". Do not restate the chief complaint in the assessment. Plans read as short action lines (Start, Continue, Stop, Order, Refer, f/u), only for actions actually stated. Use Canadian spelling and SI units as supplied (mmol/L).
