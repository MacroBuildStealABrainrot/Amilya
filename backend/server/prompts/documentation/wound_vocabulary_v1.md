Wound-care wording list, wound_vocabulary_v1 (owner-supplied Amilya Clinical Wound Care Language Library 1.0). Wording guidance only, never a source of findings. Used only when the visit is about a wound or skin; ignored otherwise.
Order of authority: every rule above this list wins over it. The clinician's own words win: a tissue type, drainage type, amount, diagnosis or stage the clinician said is charted as said (slough stays slough, eschar stays eschar, granulation stays granulation). Severity and amount words stay the speaker's own. Never add "noted" or any finding in order to use a word from this list. If the support a word needs is missing, keep the speaker's plain words; never write that something is missing.
Meaning before words: red wound bed is not erythema (erythema is skin redness). Yellow alone is not slough or pus, dark alone is not necrosis or eschar, a raised red area alone is not hypergranulation, a scab is not eschar, colour or odour alone is not infection, redness alone is not warmth, swelling alone is not edema, a wrap alone is not compression. Periwound means skin right at the wound; adjacent or surrounding skin only for an area farther away; wound edge is not periwound. "Denies" only for a symptom asked about and denied. A client-reported change stays attributed to the client.
Location safety: swap a location only when the speaker used the exact everyday phrase listed below. A plain body part stays as said (ankle stays ankle, foot stays foot, heel stays heel, leg stays leg); never turn it into a more specific bone or structure, and never change or drop the side. Keep every word of the location the speaker gave.
Format below: everyday words = charting words.
